National anthem and national song

Both were settled in one statement on 24 January 1950, two days before the Constitution took effect. Rajendra Prasad, as President of the Constituent Assembly, made no formal resolution, only this declaration:

“The composition consisting of the words and music known as Jana Gana Mana is the National Anthem of India, subject to such alterations in the words as the Government may authorise as occasion arises; and the song Vande Mataram, which has played a historic part in the struggle for Indian freedom, shall be honoured equally with Jana Gana Mana and shall have equal status with it.” Constituent Assembly Debates, 24 Jan 1950

Vande Mataram

First stanza, as the government prints it

Vande Mataram!
Sujalam, suphalam, malayaja shitalam,
Shasyashyamalam, Mataram!
Vande Mataram!
Shubhrajyotsna pulakitayaminim,
Phullakusumita drumadala shobhinim,
Suhasinim sumadhura bhashinim,
Sukhadam varadam, Mataram!
Vande Mataram, Vande Mataram!

Sri Aurobindo's prose translation

I bow to thee, Mother,
richly-watered, richly-fruited,
cool with the winds of the south,
dark with the crops of the harvests,
The Mother!
Her nights rejoicing in the glory of the moonlight,
her lands clothed beautifully with her trees in flowering bloom,
sweet of laughter, sweet of speech,
The Mother, giver of boons, giver of bliss.

Composed in Sanskrit by Bankim Chandra Chatterji (Chattopadhyay); first published in 1882 in the novel Anandamath. It was first sung on a political occasion at the 1896 session of the Indian National Congress. Portal · Wikipedia

The rules for Vande Mataram changed in 2026. News reports of a Ministry of Home Affairs order dated 28 January 2026 say the full six-stanza version, about 3 minutes 10 seconds, is now the official rendition at government events and in schools, that it is sung before Jana Gana Mana when both are performed, and that people must stand for it. Older material, including the government portal text above and the “first two verses” wording common on Wikipedia, describes the earlier practice. I could not open the order itself, so this rests on SCC Online and All India Radio, which agree with each other. The six stanzas are not reproduced here.

The flag

A horizontal tricolour of deep saffron (kesaria) at the top, white in the middle and dark green at the bottom, in equal bands. Width to length is two to three. In the centre of the white band is a navy-blue wheel with 24 spokes, its diameter about the width of the white band. The design is that of the wheel on the abacus of the Sarnath Lion Capital of Ashoka. Portal

Saffron stands for strength and courage. White stands for peace and truth. Green stands for the fertility, growth and auspiciousness of the land. Portal

The Constituent Assembly adopted this design on 22 July 1947. It is based on the Swaraj flag of the Indian National Congress, from a design by Pingali Venkayya, with the wheel replacing the charkha. Portal · Wikipedia

The Flag Code of India, 2002

In force26 January 2002Three parts: a general description; display by the public, private organizations and schools; display by governments and their agencies.Wikipedia
AmendedDecember 2021Polyester and other machine-made fabric allowed. Before this the flag had to be khadi (cotton, silk or wool, hand-spun and hand-woven).Wikipedia · news reports
Amended19 July 2022The flag may be flown day and night when displayed in the open or on a private house. Earlier it could be hoisted only between sunrise and sunset.News reports

One rule from the Code: as a mark of respect, the National Flag is never dipped to a person or thing. Regimental colors and organizational flags may be dipped. Wikipedia

Respecting the National Flag and the National Anthem is also the first of the Fundamental Duties, Article 51A(a).

National holidays and days

India has exactly three national holidays, the same everywhere in the country: Republic Day, Independence Day and Gandhi Jayanti. Every other holiday is declared by the central government for its own offices, or by each state.

National holiday26 JanuaryRepublic DayThe Constitution came into effect in 1950 and India became a republic. The date was chosen because the Indian National Congress had proclaimed Purna Swaraj, complete independence, on that day in 1930.Wikipedia
National holiday15 AugustIndependence DayIndia became independent in 1947. The Prime Minister raises the flag at the Red Fort each year and addresses the nation.Wikipedia
National holiday2 OctoberGandhi JayantiThe birthday of Mahatma Gandhi, called the Father of the Nation. The UN General Assembly made it the International Day of Non-Violence in 2007.Wikipedia

Who does what

Head of stateThe President“There shall be a President of India” (Art. 52). The Union's executive power is vested in the President, who also holds supreme command of the Defence Forces (Art. 53).Constitution
ParliamentPresident and two HousesThe Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and the House of the People (Lok Sabha) (Art. 79). The Vice-President chairs the Rajya Sabha.Constitution · Wikipedia
Lok Sabha543 elected membersThe Constitution allows up to 530 from the states and 20 from Union Territories (Art. 81). Members are directly elected for five years.Constitution · Wikipedia
Rajya Sabha245 members233 elected by the state and UT legislatures plus 12 nominated by the President for literature, science, art and social service. Article 80 allows up to 250. It is never dissolved.Constitution · Wikipedia

Language: what the Constitution actually says

“The official language of the Union shall be Hindi in Devanagari script. The form of numerals to be used for the official purposes of the Union shall be the international form of Indian numerals.” Article 343(1)

English was to continue for fifteen years (Art. 343(2)). The Official Languages Act, 1963, which took effect on 26 January 1965, kept English as an official language alongside Hindi. Court proceedings and the authoritative texts of laws are in English unless Parliament provides otherwise (Art. 348). Constitution · Wikipedia

The Constitution names no “national language”.

The Eighth Schedule lists the 22 languages the Constitution recognizes, verbatim in this order. It is a list of recognized languages, not of official or national ones. Constitution

    Rights and duties

    Part III · Articles 12–35Fundamental RightsEquality (14–18). Freedom, including speech, life and personal liberty (19–22, with 21A, the right to education for ages 6 to 14). Against exploitation (23–24). Freedom of religion (25–28). Cultural and educational rights of minorities (29–30). Constitutional remedies (32).Constitution
    Part IVA · Article 51AEleven Fundamental DutiesFrom abiding by the Constitution, the National Flag and the National Anthem, to protecting the environment and safeguarding public property. All eleven follow, verbatim.Constitution
    Article 51A, the eleven duties, verbatim

    It shall be the duty of every citizen of India—

    1. (a) to abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem;
    2. (b) to cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle for freedom;
    3. (c) to uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India;
    4. (d) to defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so;
    5. (e) to promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India transcending religious, linguistic and regional or sectional diversities; to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women;
    6. (f) to value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture;
    7. (g) to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures;
    8. (h) to develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform;
    9. (i) to safeguard public property and to abjure violence;
    10. (j) to strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement;
    11. (k) who is a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child or, as the case may be, ward between the age of six and fourteen years.

    Commonly said, but not official

    Four things most people believe, and what the government's own statements say.

    “National language”NoneThe Constitution designates an official language of the Union (Hindi in Devanagari) and English continues alongside it. It names no national language. Twenty-two languages are recognized in the Eighth Schedule.Constitution · Wikipedia
    “National sport”None declaredHockey is the usual answer and India's men's team has won eight Olympic golds, but replies to RTI queries from the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports say no sport has been declared the national game. One 2012 reply is cited on Wikipedia.Wikipedia · news reports
    “National flower”Lotus, but never notifiedThe lotus appears on the government portal. Yet in a written Rajya Sabha reply on 10 July 2019, Minister of State for Home Nityananda Rai said no notification had been issued for a national flower by the Environment Ministry.Press report · Portal
    “National reptile”King cobra: unconfirmedIt is listed as one on Wikipedia, with no adoption date. It is not among the portal's thirteen elements, and I found no official notification for it.Wikipedia
