Hair Care Regimen

A reference for the standard routine with minoxidil 5% and finasteride 1 mg: how to use each, what a day looks like, what to expect month by month, and what to watch for. Nothing here is tracked or saved.

General informationThis is typical labeled use for male pattern hair loss, not medical advice. Confirm the plan, especially finasteride, with the doctor or dermatologist who prescribed it, and follow their dose if it differs.

Topical · on the scalp

Minoxidil 5%

Stimulates follicles and lengthens the growth phase.

Dose
1 mL liquid, twice daily (foam: half a capful, twice daily)
Where
Thinning areas of a dry scalp, not the whole head
Spacing
About 12 hours apart, morning and evening
Duration
Ongoing. Gains fade a few months after stopping

Tablet · by mouth

Finasteride 1 mg

Lowers DHT, the hormone that miniaturizes follicles.

Dose
1 tablet once daily, same time each day
With food
Either way is fine
Missed dose
Skip it and carry on. Never double up
Duration
Ongoing. Loss resumes after stopping

A day on the routine

Minoxidil twice daily is the usual schedule for the 5% strength. More does not work faster.

Morning

  • Finasteride, 1 tablet
  • Minoxidil, 1 mL on a dry scalp

Evening, about 12 hours later

  • Minoxidil, 1 mL on a dry scalp
  • Ideally at least 4 hours before bed so it dries

Applying minoxidil

  1. Start with a dry scalp and dry hair.
  2. Part the hair so the liquid reaches skin, not strands.
  3. Apply the dose across the thinning area and spread it with fingertips.
  4. Wash your hands straight afterward.
  5. Let it dry fully before a hat, pillow or styling product.
  6. Leave it on at least 4 hours before washing.

Helpful extras

  • Ketoconazole 2% shampoo two or three times a week. It calms scalp irritation and may help a little itself.
  • Monthly photos in the same light, same angle, wet or dry hair kept consistent. Memory is a poor judge of slow change.
  • Microneedling is optional, about weekly at a short depth. Leave a day before minoxidil, or it can sting and absorb more.
  • Skip the dose if the scalp is broken or sunburned.

What to expect, and when

WhenWhat is normal
Weeks 2–8Shedding. Weak hairs fall as follicles reset. It looks like the treatment is failing, and it is not. Keep going.
Months 3–4Shedding settles. Finasteride starts to show as less hair in the brush and shower.
Months 4–6Minoxidil regrowth begins: fine, short hairs first, then thicker.
Months 6–12Best visible improvement builds. Finasteride reaches full effect at about 12 months.
After 1 yearThe result is maintained, not climbed further. Stopping either medicine lets the gains fade over a few months.

Individual results vary. Photos at 6 and 12 months are the fair test, so judge it then and not by the first few weeks.

Why the two together

Finasteride acts on the cause by blocking the conversion of testosterone to DHT, which slows and often stops further loss. Minoxidil acts on the follicles directly to encourage regrowth. They work by different routes, and studies show the pair outperforms either alone.

Finasteride: know this

  • A small percentage report lower libido, erection problems or low mood. Most resolve; a few report persistence after stopping.
  • It roughly halves PSA. Tell any doctor doing prostate screening that you take it.
  • Women who are or may become pregnant must not handle crushed or broken tablets.
  • Do not donate blood while taking it, and for a period after, per local blood-bank rules.

Call your doctor for any mood change, breast lumps or discharge, or sexual side effects that bother you.

Minoxidil: know this

  • Scalp irritation, dryness and flaking are common. Foam is gentler than liquid.
  • Unwanted facial or body hair can appear if it drips or is touched. Wash hands and keep it off the forehead.
  • Toxic to cats if they lick the scalp, pillow or your hands.
  • Keep it away from eyes and broken skin.

Stop and see a doctor for dizziness, a racing or irregular heartbeat, chest pain, sudden swelling of hands, feet or face, or sudden weight gain.

Written as a plain reference from standard labeling and published guidance on androgenetic alopecia. It does not diagnose hair loss, and other causes (thyroid, iron, stress, scalp conditions) need a doctor. If you are female, pregnant, planning a pregnancy or take other medicines, ask a doctor before using either one.
