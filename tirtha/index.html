Tirtha: Sacred Places of India

The traditional circuits of pilgrimage, on a map: the twelve Jyotirlingas, the Char Dham, the Chota Char Dham in the Himalaya, the seven Saptapuri cities, the five Pancha Bhoota Sthalams the six abodes of Murugan, the eighteen Maha Shakti Peethas and the 108 Divya Desams of Vishnu. Tap a marker for the deity, the place and the river beside it.

The Telugu States list (Telangana and Andhra Pradesh) follows Wikipedia's "List of Hindu temples in India" for those two states, plus the five Pancharama Kshetras. Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Maharashtra follow the same Wikipedia list, leaving out temples already shown under another circuit; it is a selection, not every temple. Where a marker says "from OpenStreetMap", the position is approximate. Circuits follow the widely used traditional lists. Some lists are disputed: three Jyotirlinga sites (Baidyanath, Nageshwar and, in some accounts, Bhimashankar) are claimed by more than one place, and this page shows one and says so. Coordinates come from Wikipedia and OpenStreetMap and place the marker at the temple or town, not surveyed to the metre. The 18 Maha Shakti Peethas and the 108 Divya Desams are taken from Wikipedia's articles "Shakta pithas" and "Divya Desam". Other traditions list 51, 64 or 108 Shakti Peethas, so this is one list of several. Divya Desam no. 107 (Thiruparkadal) and no. 108 (Vaikuntha) are in heaven and cannot be mapped. Where Wikipedia gave a wrong or missing location for a Divya Desam I placed it at the town from OpenStreetMap or Wikipedia, and shrines marked approximate are only at town or district level. Numbers 20 and 34 each cover more than one shrine, so the map has 109 Divya Desam markers. Map tiles © OpenStreetMap contributors.
